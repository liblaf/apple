Controlled 2D evidence

Why the muscle layer curls

The loss observes the free top boundary. Interior geometry is not observed. Giving every active triangle its own unbounded 3-DoF activation lets the optimizer create locally incompatible maps that improve the top fit while folding the muscle underneath.

muscle + fat only bottom and sides fixed top and interior free no skin no activation bounds no inversion barrier no contact or repair

Two flips can hide inside one elastic determinant

For an active element, det G = det F × det A⁻¹. A physical flip and an activation-map flip can multiply back to a positive elastic determinant. The Stable Neo-Hookean term therefore has no built-in reason to reject every visibly folded state.

det F < 0 physical element flips
det A⁻¹ < 0 activation map flips too
det G > 0 the product becomes positive again

Factor study

What changes the bumpiness?

Target height has the clearest matched trend. Loss, mesh, material model, length, muscle extent, sharing, and Poisson ratio all change the result, but none by itself is a universal cure.

Target height

The lower upward parabola is much smoother and ends orientation-positive. Doubling the height makes folding far more severe. None of the three height endpoints passed the practical-stationarity gate.

h=.025 · detF +.479 h=.050 · detF −11.04 h=.100 · detF −45.33

Loss

L1 finishes smoother than the L2 baseline; L∞ finishes rougher. L1 and L∞ still end inverted and nonstationary, so a norm change does not remove the mechanism.

L1 roughness .000692 L2 roughness .001202 L∞ roughness .001625

Mesh resolution

The 50×5 case is smooth and stationary. The 200×20 case runs the full Adam + L-BFGS protocol, has zero solver failures, yet remains nonstationary and inverted. More cells also mean more independent controls and harder conditioning.

50×5 · detF +.640 100×10 · detF −11.04 200×20 · detF −21.45

Elasticity

Linear elasticity is smooth and stationary in this matched run; Stable Neo-Hookean is not. Linear elasticity still crosses a negative determinant during its trajectory, so this is not a physical guarantee.

linear · detF +.009 Stable NH · detF −11.04 ν fixed at .49

Length and muscle extent

A short 0.1 × 0.1 body is not automatically safer: keeping h=.05 makes its target 10× steeper and 100× more curved than the 1 × 0.1 target. Full-muscle cases are smoother on average, but a stationary full/per-cell case still ends inverted.

Poisson ratio

ν=.35 and ν=.49 change fold incidence, area, and local severity in different directions. Compressibility changes the symptom; it does not remove the unconstrained local activation route.

Direction check

What if the target is pushed down?

Downward motion is not the upward result with its sign reversed. The fixed-boundary nonlinear problem selects a different branch.

The downward case reaches only 38.5% of the requested center displacement and has about 12.9× the upward target RMS. It transiently inverts from steps 29–105, then finishes orientation-positive with min detF=.0729. Both endpoints are horizon-limited.

Interpretation

What the evidence does—and does not—say

Supported

Independent, unbounded activation provides a concrete route to folded muscle and rough visible geometry. Shared activation and full-muscle controls usually improve coherence, with a fit tradeoff. The same double-inversion sequence occurred in the historical bumpy face muscle section.

Not established: inversion count is not monotone with visible bumpiness. The historically smoother skin + tightening comparator actually has more active-muscle flipped cells (974 vs 110) and more zygomaticus double inversions (24 vs 4). Skin, prestrain, force transmission, fold location, branch selection, and optimizer history can all change what reaches the surface.

Both saved best checkpoints contain a double-inverted tetrahedron; the source inverse runs were not converged. Open the full matched figure.

Takeaway: the optimizer can fit what we observe while folding what we do not. The 2D model demonstrates the mechanism under controlled changes; the face history confirms its presence, not exclusivity.
