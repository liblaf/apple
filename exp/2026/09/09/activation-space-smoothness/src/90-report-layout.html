Activation models and the volume-energy correction

A smoother active-stress result led us to an error in the volume energy. Correcting it also made the original activation baseline much smoother. The later axis and smoothness tests produced limited further improvement.

All experiments below have no skin energy.The face surface is used for fitting and visualization. The baseline and active-stress comparisons have no inverse regularization; a spatial penalty is introduced in step 4.

1Active stress first produced a smoother face

The old six-parameter activation baseline fitted the target closely but produced pronounced bumps. PSD active stress gave a visibly smoother result without a spatial regularizer. This initial observation motivated the mathematical comparison.

2The expected equivalence exposed a determinant error

Let B = A⁻¹ be the activation multiplier in the code, so the activated gradient is G = FB. With Q = μ(BBᵀ − I) and physical-volume energy, the activation and active-stress formulations satisfy:

Wactivation(F, B) = W0(F) + ½ Q : (FᵀF)
= Wstress(F, Q) + ½ tr Q

The difference is independent of deformation. Mapped controls therefore give the same equilibrium forces and deformation Hessian. But the old implementation used det(FB) in the determinant terms. Since det(FB) = det(F) det(B), activation could keep that quantity near one while physical tissue volume changed.

The correction: keep the activated norm ‖FB‖², but use J = det F in both determinant terms.
W = μ/2 (‖FB‖² − 3) − μ(J − 1) + λ0/2 (J − 1)²,   J = det F

This is forward equivalence under a control mapping. The tested admissible spaces differ: unrestricted Raw6 permits negative effective modes, PSD excludes them, and learned axis is rank one. Their inverse optimization histories are not equivalent. Independent material validation.

3The corrected baseline also became much smoother

We reran the six-parameter activation model with zero initialization, no inverse regularization, and the same 200-update budget. The corrected energy already removes much of the old surface bumpiness. Its mouth and muscle deformation are close to the active-stress result.

Isolated tracked tetrahedron at a common scale

Across the active muscle cohort, the old RMS deviations from one are 0.145 for det(FB) and 0.432 for det F. The correction is a major confound in the initial active-stress interpretation. The corrected result still has one inverted mixed cell containing 99.9023% fat, and residual surface irregularities remain. These are not matched-fit or fully converged superiority claims.

4Axis constraints and smoothness added little improvement

We then tested learned uniaxial contraction, B = I + vvᵀ, and a same-muscle spatial smoothness penalty on C = B − I. The selected learned-axis result fits more closely but retains more inverted cells and localized corrugation. It does not establish a further improvement in geometric quality.

Controlled smoothness test: almost unchanged shape and field

At update 200, the Raw6 off/on shapes and dominant activation fields look nearly indistinguishable. Smoothing lowers S(C) by 11.0%, but improves the local surface residual by only 0.20%. The matched learned-axis snapshots show a similarly small 0.40% residual reduction.
